# Installation & Structural Engineering Guide

> *Synthetic Demo Knowledge Document for CraftFlow Development*

## Architectural & Structural Planning

Installing a monolithic natural stone bathtub requires early coordination between the client, interior architect, general contractor, and structural engineer.

### 1. Structural Load Assessment
- Empty Tub Weight: 600 kg to 800 kg.
- Full Water Capacity: ~300 to 350 Litres (approx. 300 to 350 kg).
- Occupant Allowance: ~100 kg.
- **Total Combined Load:** Up to 1,200 kg (2,645 lbs).
- **Floor Requirement:** The finished subfloor must withstand concentrated dead loads and dynamic loads of at least 4.5 kN/m² to 5.0 kN/m² (approx. 95 to 105 lbs/sq ft) with a maximum allowable deflection of L/720.

### 2. Access Pathway & Rigging
Before dispatch, contractors must verify the complete transit pathway from delivery curb to final bathroom placement:
- Elevator weight capacities and cabin dimensions.
- Staircase turns, landing widths, and banister clearances.
- Doorway widths (minimum 900 mm clear opening recommended).
- Crane hoisting or exterior scaffolding may be required for penthouse and upper-floor installations.

### 3. Subfloor Preparation
The bathtub must rest on a completely flat, rigid, non-deflecting substrate (reinforced concrete slab or doubled 25 mm marine plywood with steel joist sisters). Mortar leveling bed or high-density neoprene isolation pads (6 mm thickness) are placed underneath the perimeter base to distribute weight evenly and prevent point-loading pressure points.

### 4. Plumbing Hookup
- **Drain Assembly:** 2-inch (50 mm) or 1.5-inch (38 mm) brass waste outlet.
- **Trap Access:** A low-profile P-trap with access panel from ceiling below or side inspection panel is strongly recommended.
- **Tub Fillers:** We recommend freestanding floor-mounted deck fillers positioned with a minimum spout reach of 220 mm to clear the bathtub rim bevel.
